# TerraSync Code – Satellite-Driven Crop Rotation & Soil Resilience Decision System
NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data · Team TERRASYNC CODE

Static platform (HTML/CSS/JS + Leaflet + Chart.js) with Moquegua (Peru) pilot case and live analysis of any coordinate
(NASA POWER + ISRIC SoilGrids + Open-Meteo terrain elevation). English interface by default, EN/ES button.

## Structure
- `ingest_data.py` – reads `datos_nasa/`, queries APIs, creates `terrasync.db` (4 tables), and exports `data/moquegua_data.json` and `data/moquegua_data.js`.
- `index.html`, `styles.css`, `app.js` – web application.
- `datos_nasa/` – your real files (POWER CSV, SoilGrids JSON, AppEEARS SMAP and NDVI CSVs). Any new `*SMAP*NDVI*.csv` is detected automatically.

## Usage
1. `python ingest_data.py` (with internet: downloads NASA POWER and SoilGrids for Samegua, Torata, and Omate; use `--offline` to skip APIs).
2. `python -m http.server 8000` and open http://localhost:8000 (also works by opening index.html directly, thanks to `moquegua_data.js`).
3. Publish: upload the folder to GitHub Pages or Vercel (100% static site).

## Decision Modules
- **Global 30-crop library:** cereals and pseudocereals, legumes, roots and tubers, perennials/fruit trees, and vegetables/specialty crops. Allows searching, filtering by category/status, selecting a crop to synchronize the calendar and simulator, and adding custom crops. The pH, temperature, cycle, water, N-fixation, and drought tolerance parameters are approximate general agronomic traits, FAO-style; they are not satellite observations or local calibration. Custom crops are saved in the browser's `localStorage`.
- The cards use original SVG illustrations generated by the application for all 30 crops and categories. They do not load photographs, external images, or third-party licensed resources; they also work offline and when opened with `file:///`. Custom crops reuse an illustration from their category.
- **Crop summary and traffic-light board:** filters the library using SoilGrids pH, estimated aridity, NASA POWER days below the crop's temperature thresholds, and selected water availability. Each color displays reasons and values; red indicates a mismatch with this filter, not a definitive agronomic recommendation.
- **Adaptive 12-month calendar:** groups available monthly normals of POWER Tmin, Tmax, precipitation, and water stress. When changing the active crop, it applies its general frost/heat and water thresholds and calculates an indicative sequence using its `cycle_months`. Months without sufficient observations remain unclassified. The windows are not observed crop calendars or local recommendations.
- **Adaptive six-cycle / three-year simulator:** compares continuous monoculture of the active crop against that crop plus a complementary partner selected by nitrogen fixation, drought tolerance, water, and local traffic-light status, for precipitation (-30% to +20%), temperature (0 to +3 °C), and efficiency (50% / 85%). Its indices use deterministic rules and general assumptions, not a machine learning model, measured yield, or guarantee. The soil index starts from available SOC (`40 + 2 × SOC`, capped at 20–80) or 50; pilot SMAP and NDVI only slightly modify stress, and their mixed pixels are not plot-level measurements.
- **Local plot logbook:** saves name, area, previous crop, date, notes, and a site snapshot (coordinates, aridity, pH, and top strategy) in `localStorage`. Allows inspecting/loading, deleting, and exporting all records as CSV or JSON. Data remains in the browser; clearing local site data deletes it.
- **Observation coverage:** distinguishes the sources available for the active site (POWER, SMAP, Terra MODIS, SoilGrids, and elevation) from products not yet integrated (GPM IMERG, ECOSTRESS, Landsat 8/9, Sentinel-2, GLDAS, and FAO GAEZ). SMAP and MODIS are only included for the pilot zones.

## Elevation and Serverless Operation
- Each analysis queries `https://api.open-meteo.com/v1/elevation` for terrain elevation (90 m global DEM based on SRTM/Copernicus, served by Open-Meteo). If the call fails, the site displays unknown elevation and keeps the NASA POWER/MERRA-2 climate grid cell elevation separately; it does not conflate them.
- The protected bundle `data/moquegua_data.js` continues loading before `app.js`. The app accepts `window.MOQUEGUA_DATA` and the existing name `window.TERRASYNC_DATA`, assigning an in-memory alias to preserve `file:///` opening without modifying or regenerating the data.
- EN is the default language; all added labels have ES translations. Unavailable sources and values are indicated in the interface.

## Data Fidelity (Important)
- The included POWER CSV contains `ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DNI` (direct normal irradiance), not `ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN`; 71 trailing days with -999 are stored as NULL. Radiation is not used in any calculation.
- The CSV's MERRA-2 grid cell has a mean elevation of 3851 m: temperatures (mean ≈ 7.9 °C) do not represent the valley floor. The app states this under "Data gaps".
- Open-Meteo terrain elevation is a separate source from the reanalysis cell's mean elevation; the comparison does not adjust or replace NASA POWER temperatures.
- No data is fabricated or corrected. Missing values appear as "n/a" or with a notice. ET₀ (Hargreaves-Samani), aridity index, and stress index are derived estimates and are labeled accordingly.
- SMAP/NDVI only exist for the 4 pilot zones; for other points, modeled MERRA-2 soil wetness (GWETTOP) is used.
- Strategy scores are a transparent heuristic; crop attributes are general agronomic references to be validated with an agronomist.
